# Student Resource: Personal Narrative Guide

In an interview or networking conversation, you will often be prompted with statements like "Tell me about yourself", "What are you interested in?", or "How did you get into this field?" Some people struggle to answer these questions clearly and succinctly because they have never practiced describing themselves in a professional way, or because they do not understand the purpose of these questions. This guide is meant to help you describe your own personal story and understand that you do not need to be deep into your career to have a meaningful narrative to share.

## What Is a Personal Narrative?

A personal narrative is a short, intentional, and structured explanation of who you are. Your story will generally include what you are working towards and what experiences you have had that have helped you get to where you are currently. Importantly, your personal narrative will not and cannot be your full life story! Part of the reason we practice preparing the narrative is to make sure we narrow down the details of our life to the most important bullets that the interviewer or networking partner would want to know. The narrative will also not be just a list of your accomplishments. The narrative should allow someone to get to know who you are and what matters to you.

A personal narrative is key for professional conversations, but it should also sound authentic when you share it. Describe yourself and your story in your own words. **Do not use LLMs to generate your personal story!** The tone will sound generic and unfocused. Be yourself and let the world know who you truly are.

## Personal Narrative Structure

**1\. Present: Where You Are Now**

- Your major or focus area
- What you're currently working on

**2\. Past: What Led You Here**

- A class, project, experience, or moment
- What sparked your interest

**3\. Future: Where You're Headed**

- What you want to explore or grow into
- What you're curious about next

**Example**

"I'm a junior computer science major interested in web development. I took an internship last year working on web design and some back-end web development and realized I enjoy problem-solving and building solutions. I'm now looking for jobs where I can continue developing my technical skills and do some front-end design work as well."

## "Tell Me About Yourself"

This is one of the most common ways to begin an interview, but it is often intimidating to students and early-career professionals, as it is open-ended and fairly vague. When presenting this question, interviewers are really asking how you describe yourself. What about you should they know, especially if it is something not explicitly a bullet point on your resume. Interviewers are also generally judging your ability to communicate with this question. Can you answer the question succinctly, or does your answer tend to ramble without a clear direction?

If you don't know where to start, consider following this 3-sentence structure as a first option for your answer.

- I am \[year and major\] at \[university\]
- I am interested in \[part of your program or job description that you are focusing on\]
- I am working toward \[goal\]

For example, "I am currently a junior computer science major at Carlow University. Last year I completed an internship with our Marketing Department, which helped me realize I am really interested in web design and development. I am currently looking for opportunities where I can apply what I have learned and continue to expand my back-end development skills."

Remember, you want this answer to sound authentic and conversational when you answer it in a real job setting. It is a good idea to practice answers to common interview questions prior to your interview to increase your comfort and decrease your anxiety, but you do not want to sound rehearsed. You also do not want to sound like someone else! Adapt the script to sound authentically like your voice.

Try to keep your answer relatively brief in the beginning. Your interviewer may ask follow-up questions if they want more details. You also do not want to answer with only a single sentence, especially if that sentence is information you could already find on your resume ("I am a junior at Carlow.") Give them some details about your interests, motivations, and/or goals that can help them to understand how you would contribute to their organization.

## Describe Your Professional Experiences

While internships are some of the easiest ways to describe professional experience in tech, not everyone has past internships to discuss. (Everyone has to start somewhere!) If you are struggling to think of what you can describe on an interview, think about other things you may have done during college:

- Class projects (individual or in groups)
- Personal projects
- Part-time jobs
- Research
- Volunteering

While not every workplace will value each of these equally, they can all point towards a well-rounded, professional student with experience worthy of sharing.

### How to Talk About Your Experience

Think about each professional experience in terms of three major bullets:

- What you did
- What you learned
- Why it mattered to you

For example, "I completed a semester-long final project in my Algorithms course in which I designed and tested an algorithm to clean and process raw data. I learned a lot of technical skills around data analysis and discovered that I am really interested in this aspect of data science. I am hoping to get more involved in data collection and processing in future projects as well."

Make a list of your projects and work experiences, and for each, think about how you would answer these three bullet points. Consider practicing these discussions with your mentor. Your mentor may ask additional follow-up questions about some of your experiences to give you practice making the discussion more natural and authentic, as a real interview would be.

## Talking About Growth (Not Perfection)

Remember, your interviewer knows you are an early-career job seeker. You are not expected to know everything or be an expert in anything at this point! It is okay to be honest about what you do not know, but you should also emphasize where and how you have grown in your career and your enthusiasm for continuing to grow in this role in the future. Employers value a willingness to learn and engage thoughtfully. Use your answers to demonstrate curiosity, effort, passion, dedication, and growth.

"I am still developing my skills as a web developer, but I am passionate about good user experiences. I am a self-motivated learner and would be excited to learn from my colleagues and ask questions when I am unsure about something."
