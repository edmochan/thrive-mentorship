# Resume and Cover Letter Tips

## 1\. Technical/CS Cover Letter Best Practices

#### What is included on a cover letter

- Your cover letter should tell your story beyond just the facts listed on your resume. Use your cover letter to sell yourself and explain clearly why you are applying to this position.
- Always customize your cover letter for each job to which you apply. Include relevant details about yourself and details from the job posting.
- Review your letter for typos or other errors. Consider having it reviewed by the Office of Career Development, your faculty advisor, your mentor, or a writing tutor before submitting it.

#### Tips and reminders

- DO NOT copy example cover letters exactly, and avoid fully AI-generated letters. Your letter should speak to who you are. Letters copied from other sources will be too generic to market you as an individual.
- Remind the students that cover letters should be customized to each role and each company.
- Encourage them to make an appointment with Carlow's Office of Career Development if they would like another set of eyes on their writing.

## 2\. Technical/CS Resume Best Practices

#### What is included on a resume

- Contact info
  - Full name
  - Location (city and state – you do not need to write your entire address)
  - Phone number (and make sure your voicemail message is professional)
  - Email address (and make sure you regularly check this email! Use a professional email address)
  - (optional) links to LinkedIn, GitHub, or other online portfolio
- Education
  - University, degree, and planned graduation date (i.e. Bachelor of Science in Computer Science, Carlow University, expected May 2029)
  - (optional) minors, GPA (if above 3.5), Honors status, or other academic achievements
  - (optional) 1-2 lines of **relevant** coursework. As you grow in your career, you will delete this line to make room for work experience.
- Skills, Software, and Certifications
  - List skills relevant to the position, in decreasing order of importance. You will want to customize this list for each application.
  - If you have completed any additional certifications, add them here.
- Work Experience
  - List experiences in reverse chronological order (most recent first)
  - Use bullets to describe what you did at the job. Be specific! Start each bullet with a verb and include accomplishments and successes.
- Projects
  - List projects relevant to the position, in decreasing order of importance. You will want to customize this list for each application.
  - Use bullets to describe what you did in the project. Be specific! Start each bullet with a verb and be clear about what you accomplished and how you did so.
  - If your project is posted in an online repository, provide a direct link. Make sure the link directs to an active, accessible site with a clear and well-writtten README.
  - Align projects with skills listed.
  - As you gain work experience, the Projects section can shrink accordingly.
- (optional) Leadership Experience, Volunteer Experience, Teaching Experience, Activities, Honors and Awards, etc.
  - If you are new to the workforce, these can help add to your resume.
  - Use bullets and include the location and date(s), as needed.
  - List these only if they are relevant to the position.

#### Tips and recommendations

- Keep your resume limited to 1 page, with the most important information at the top.
- Avoid templates with a lot of color or unusual fonts. Keep the resume simple and easy to read. Use only traditional fonts and minimize colors.
- Use bullet points rather than blocks of writing. Assume someone will be skimming your resume and will spend under a minute reading it. You want the important information easily visible!
- Discuss your work experience with specific, measurable outcomes. Quantify outcomes where you can and provide details so the reviewer can get to know you and your accomplishments.
- Avoid generic language in favor of specific and unique examples.
- Tailor your resume to each job you apply to. Look for keywords in the job description and make sure you use that language in your resume. Prioritize your list of skills and projects based on the job.
- Check your resume for typos. Consider having it reviewed by the Office of Career Development, your faculty advisor, your mentor, or a writing tutor before submitting it.
- Save your resume as a PDF before submitting. Use a filename that includes your first and last name. DO NOT submit a resume called "resume.docx" or another generic title. You want someone to be able to find your documents later!

## 3\. Technical and Behavioral Interviews

#### Preparing for a technical interview

- Review the job posting and note the skills, software, and technologies the job requires.
- Review your class notes on data structures, algorithms, and other technologies required by the job. Use other outside resources to learn and review as needed.
- **Practice!** There are a lot of online resources with practice technical questions,. Expect to have to complete a task or describe how you would solve a problem.
- Review your previous projects and coursework and practice explaining the process you used to solve a problem and complete a task. Your process matters as much as your solution!
- Ask your mentor to practice interviewing with you and provide constructive feedback.
- Interviews can be stressful and intimidating. The more you practice and prepare ahead of time, the more confident you will feel.

#### Preparing for a behavioral/non-technical interview

- Know your resume inside and out. Be prepared to discuss your qualifications and weaknesses. Practice common interview questions and be prepared to provide answers specific to you as an applicant. (Don't just be ready to provide generic answers!)
- Ask your mentor or staff in the Office of Career Development to practice interviewing with you and provide constructive feedback.
- Prepare some anecdotes prior to the interview to share in response to common interview topics (teamwork, success, failure, leadership, etc). Anecdotes can often be framed in the START method (Situation, Task, Action, Result, Takeaway) to fully address an interview question.
